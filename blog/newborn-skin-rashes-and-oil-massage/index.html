Newborn skin rashes, peeling and blue patches, and which oil for massage

Most spots, rashes and peeling on a newborn's skin are normal and fade on their own. Blue-grey patches on the back are very common in Indian babies and are harmless. Gentle massage is fine, but Dr Dutta advises against mustard oil and against putting oil in the nose or ears.

What are the tiny white spots on my newborn's nose?

Tiny white or yellow spots on the nose and cheeks are usually milia or sebaceous gland hyperplasia. Both are harmless and fade without treatment over the first weeks.

Milia are small pockets of trapped keratin, a skin protein. They appeared in about 8 percent of babies in a large American study.1 Sebaceous gland hyperplasia (tiny oil glands) is even more common. An Indian study of 1000 newborns found it in almost 9 out of 10 babies.2 Similar white spots on the gums or roof of the mouth, called Epstein pearls, were just as common.2 Please do not squeeze any of them.

What is the white coating on my baby's skin at birth?

The white, creamy coating is vernix. It is a natural moisturiser and protective layer made of water, fats and proteins.

Leave it alone. It soaks in or rubs off over a day or two. WHO advises waiting at least 24 hours after birth before the first bath.3 Bathing too early can chill the baby and strips away this layer. When you do bathe, use plain water or a mild, fragrance-free cleanser, keep it short, and dry the neck folds and groin well.

What is the red blotchy rash with yellow spots on my newborn?

This is most likely erythema toxicum neonatorum. Despite its scary name, it is harmless and fades within a week or two.

It usually appears on day 2 or 3 as red blotches, often with a small yellow or white bump in the middle. It comes and goes on the face, chest, back and limbs, and moves around over hours. In the Indian study of 1000 newborns, almost 1 in 4 babies had it.2 The baby feeds well and has no fever.

What makes Dr Dutta look more closely: blisters filled with fluid or pus, a rash in a baby with fever, or a baby who seems unwell.

Is newborn acne normal?

Yes. Small red pimples on the cheeks, nose and forehead, starting a few weeks after birth, are common and settle on their own.

They are thought to be linked to normal hormone changes and skin yeasts. Wash the face with plain water and pat dry. Do not use adult acne creams, oils or home pastes. If the spots are crusted, oozing, spread widely or last for months, show them to your paediatrician.

What is prickly heat in babies and how do I prevent it?

Prickly heat (miliaria) is a rash of tiny clear or red bumps caused by blocked sweat ducts. It is very common in hot, humid weather and usually clears within days once the baby is cooler.

It shows up on the neck, upper chest, back and in skin folds. In the Indian newborn study, about 3 percent of babies had the clear blister form.2 The most common cause Dr Dutta sees is overdressing. Grandmothers often wrap babies in several layers, even in a Kolkata June. To prevent it:

  • Dress for the room. WHO's general advice is 1 or 2 more layers of clothing than an adult is wearing.3 In hot, humid weather, use the lower end: one light cotton layer more than the adults in the room.
  • Keep the room airy, with a fan running gently
  • Avoid thick creams and oils in skin folds

Why is my newborn's skin peeling?

Dry, peeling skin on the hands, feet and ankles in the first one to two weeks is normal. It is the outer layer of skin adjusting from life in water to life in air.

The Indian newborn study found peeling in about 1 in 10 babies.2 It is often more striking in babies born after their due date. It does not mean the baby is dehydrated or short of vitamins. If the cracks look sore, a small amount of plain, fragrance-free moisturiser is fine. Otherwise it settles on its own.

What are the blue-grey patches on my baby's back?

Flat, blue-grey patches on the lower back and bottom are called congenital dermal melanocytosis, still often called Mongolian spots. They are harmless, and most fade over the first years.

They are very common in Indian babies. In the study of 1000 Indian newborns, about 85 percent had them.2 They come from pigment cells that stayed deep in the skin before birth. One practical tip: make sure they are noted in your baby's health records. Doctors unfamiliar with them have sometimes mistaken them for bruises, which is upsetting for any family.

What are the pink patches on my baby's eyelids or neck?

Pink or red flat patches on the eyelids, between the eyebrows or at the back of the neck are salmon patches, also called stork bites. They are harmless.

They are among the most common birthmarks. In the Indian newborn study, about 1 in 5 babies had them.2 In an American study they were found in most babies.1 Patches on the face usually fade in the first year or two. Those on the back of the neck may stay but are hidden by hair.

Is a strawberry birthmark dangerous?

Most strawberry marks (infantile haemangiomas) are harmless and shrink over time, but some need early treatment. They occur in up to 5 percent of infants.4

They are often not visible at birth and show up as a small red spot in the first weeks. They grow fastest between 1 and 3 months, and most stop growing by 5 months.4 Early review, ideally by 1 month of age, is advised for haemangiomas that could cause problems.4 These include ones near the eye, on the lip or nose, in the nappy area, large ones on the face, or ones that break down and bleed. Effective medicine is available, and timing matters.

Why are my newborn's hands and feet blue?

Bluish hands and feet in the first days are common and normal. This is called acrocyanosis, and it happens because blood flow to the hands and feet is still settling.

The Indian newborn study found it in about 3 in 10 babies.2 It often shows when the baby is cold or after a bath, and improves with warmth. Blue colour of the lips, tongue or face is different. That is a danger sign and needs a doctor at once.

Is oil massage good for newborns?

Gentle massage can be good for babies. WHO says parents may consider a gentle massage of the whole body for healthy term babies.3

In most Indian homes, daily massage starts in the first weeks, often by the grandmother or a hired malishwali. The touch, eye contact and routine help bonding, and many babies sleep well afterwards. In premature babies, massage was linked with better weight gain in a Cochrane review, though the studies had quality problems.5 So Dr Dutta is happy for families to continue massage. The questions are which oil, and how.

Is mustard oil safe for baby massage?

Dr Dutta advises against mustard oil for newborns. In laboratory tests, mustard oil damaged the skin barrier and slowed its repair.6

In the same work, sunflower seed oil helped the barrier recover faster.6 In Bengal, mustard oil warmed with garlic is a deeply loved tradition, and Dr Dutta understands why families want it. But a newborn's skin barrier is still maturing, and in her clinic she often sees red, rashy skin after mustard oil massage. Choose a gentler oil instead.

Which oil is best for baby massage?

For a newborn, she usually suggests coconut oil or plain sunflower oil, in small amounts. The evidence for any oil in healthy term babies is limited.

  • Coconut oil: in an Indian trial, coconut oil massage was linked with better weight gain in premature babies.7
  • Sunflower seed oil: in very premature babies in Bangladesh, it reduced hospital infections by about 40 percent.8
  • Olive oil: a UK pilot trial suggested both olive and sunflower oil may affect the skin's fat layers in healthy newborns, and urged caution.9

Warm your hands, not the oil. Avoid broken or inflamed skin, and stop if a rash appears.

Should I put oil in my baby's nose or ears?

No. Oil dropped into the nose can be breathed into the lungs and cause lipoid pneumonia, a stubborn lung inflammation.

Doctors in South India described this many years ago in babies whose noses and throats were cleaned with oil.10 A review of children worldwide found it is still being reported, often linked to cultural practices.11 Oil in the ears does not prevent infection and can irritate the ear. Nothing should go into your baby's nose, ears or eyes.

Is baby powder safe for newborns?

Dr Dutta advises families not to use talcum or baby powder. Babies can breathe in the fine dust, and inhaled baby powder has been reported as a hazard to babies' lungs.12

Powder also cakes in skin folds and around the cord stump, where it can trap moisture. For nappy rash, she prefers a thin layer of a zinc-based barrier cream at each change. For prickly heat, cooler clothing works better than powder.

Which baby skin care products are safe for newborns?

For most newborns, less is more. Plain water, a mild fragrance-free cleanser for the nappy area, and a plain moisturiser for dry patches are enough.

WHO does not recommend routinely applying creams or oils to healthy newborns to prevent skin problems.3 When you buy products:

  • Choose fragrance-free and colour-free
  • Avoid products with many plant extracts or essential oils
  • Test a small patch first and wait a day
  • Avoid talcum powder and kajal

When should I show my baby's skin to a doctor?

Please see a doctor if your baby has:

  • Blisters or pus-filled spots, especially in clusters
  • Any rash with fever, poor feeding or unusual sleepiness
  • Yellow skin that reaches the palms and soles3
  • Spreading redness, or red skin around the cord
  • Purple or bruise-like spots that do not fade when you press a glass against them
  • A birthmark that grows quickly, bleeds, or sits near the eye, lip or nose4
  • Blue lips, tongue or face

Go the same day if your baby seems unwell along with the rash.
