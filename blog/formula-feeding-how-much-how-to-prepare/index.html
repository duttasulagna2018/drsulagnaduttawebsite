How much formula does a baby need, and how to make a bottle safely

Safe preparation matters just as much: very hot boiled water, clean bottles, and the exact scoop count on the tin.

How much formula should a newborn drink?

A newborn usually drinks about 30 to 60 ml per feed in the first week, rising to about 90 to 120 ml per feed by one month.1

On day one, a baby's stomach is tiny, so the first feeds are small. The amount creeps up each day. If your baby finishes a bottle quickly and still roots and smacks her lips, offer a little more next time. If she pushes the teat away or turns her head, she has had enough.

Write the feeds in a notebook for the first two weeks. It helps you see the pattern, and it helps Dr Dutta at the first check-up.

How much formula does a baby need by weight?

A rough guide is about 75 ml of formula a day for every pound of body weight, which is close to 165 ml per kg, spread across the day's feeds.1

This average comes from the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP).1 By this guide, a 4 kg baby may take around 660 ml in 24 hours. Most babies do not need more than about 960 ml a day.1 If your baby seems to want much more than this, talk to your paediatrician before simply adding bottles.

Use these numbers as a check, not a target. Babies have hungry days and lazy days, especially in a hot Kolkata summer when they may want smaller, more frequent feeds.

How often should a formula-fed baby eat?

Most formula-fed babies settle into a fairly regular pattern of a feed every 3 to 4 hours.1

Formula takes a little longer to digest than breast milk, so the gaps are often longer than for a breastfed baby. In the first few weeks, do not let your baby sleep more than 4 to 5 hours without a feed. Wake her gently and offer a bottle.1

After the first month, feed by cues rather than by the clock. A fixed rigid schedule is not needed for a healthy term baby.

How do I know if my baby is hungry or full?

A baby who drains the bottle and keeps smacking her lips may still be hungry. A baby who gets fidgety or easily distracted during a feed has probably had enough.1

Crying is a late hunger sign, so try to feed before it gets to that stage. During a feed, watch your baby, not the markings on the bottle. If she loses interest, she is probably done, even if milk is left.

Grandmothers often worry when a bottle is not finished. Please do not force the last 20 ml. Respecting fullness is one of the best habits you can build from the start.

What are the signs of overfeeding a formula-fed baby?

Signs of overfeeding include frequent large spit-ups, a tight swollen tummy, fussiness after feeds and weight gain that races ahead of the growth chart.

Formula-fed babies are more likely than breastfed babies to gain weight too fast, and fast early weight gain is linked with obesity later in childhood.2 A review of feeding studies suggested some simple habits that may help: do not overfeed, do not add cereal to the bottle, and do not let your baby go to sleep with a bottle.2

Let your baby set the pace. Hold the bottle fairly level so milk does not pour in, and pause every few minutes. A slow-flow teat suits most young babies.

What water should I use to make formula?

Use fresh tap or filtered water that has been brought to a full boil, and mix the powder while the water is still very hot, around 70 degrees C.3

Powdered formula is not sterile. It can rarely carry a germ called Cronobacter, which can cause serious infection in young babies.3 Very hot water kills it. For babies under 2 months, babies born early, and babies with weak immunity, boil the water and let it cool for only about 5 minutes before mixing.3

Do not use water from a roadside tank or an old bottle that has been opened. In Kolkata, Dr Dutta suggests boiling water fresh for each batch rather than relying on a water purifier alone.

How do I prepare a formula bottle step by step?

Wash your hands, use a clean bottle, pour in the measured hot water first, then add the exact number of level scoops the tin says.3

  1. Wash your hands with soap and clean the kitchen counter.3
  2. Boil fresh water and let it cool briefly, as above.
  3. Pour the water into the bottle first, up to the line you need.3
  4. Add level scoops of powder using only the scoop from that tin. Do not pack it down.
  5. Close the bottle and shake well until no lumps remain.
  6. Cool it under cold running water. Test a few drops on the inside of your wrist. It should feel warm, not hot.3

Never add extra water to make the tin last longer, and never add extra powder to "make the baby stronger". Too much water means your baby does not get enough nutrition. Too little water makes her kidneys and gut work too hard.3

How do I clean and sterilise baby bottles at home?

Wash bottles, teats and caps in hot soapy water with a bottle brush after every feed, then sterilise them, for example by boiling.3

Milk left in the teat is the perfect food for germs, especially in Kolkata's heat and monsoon humidity. Rinse straight after the feed so the milk does not dry. Then scrub, rinse, and sterilise. Boiling in a covered pan, a steam steriliser or a sterilising solution all work. Let everything air-dry on a clean cloth and store it covered.

Replace teats that are cracked, sticky or torn. Keep a separate brush just for baby bottles.

Can I prepare formula bottles in advance?

It is safest to make each bottle fresh, but if you must, put a freshly made bottle straight in the fridge and use it within 24 hours.3

Prepared formula spoils at room temperature. Use it within 2 hours of making it and within 1 hour of starting the feed.3 Throw away whatever your baby leaves in the bottle. Saliva mixed with milk lets germs grow fast.3

For outings, Dr Dutta prefers this plan: carry boiled water in a clean flask that keeps it very hot, plus pre-measured powder in a clean dry box. Mix when your baby is hungry. Write the date on a tin when you open it, as most formula should be used within a month of opening.3

Can I warm a formula bottle in the microwave?

No. Microwaves heat milk unevenly and can leave hot spots that burn a baby's mouth and throat.3

Formula does not need to be warm at all, but many babies prefer it. Stand the bottle in a bowl of warm water, or hold it under warm running tap water, for a few minutes. Shake it, then always test a few drops on your wrist.

Which formula is best, and is expensive formula better?

For a healthy term baby, a standard "stage 1" infant formula from a sealed, in-date tin is enough. In Dr Dutta's experience, a higher price does not mean a healthier baby.

Before you buy, look at the tin carefully:

  • It should say infant formula for 0 to 6 months (or "stage 1").
  • Check the "use by" date. Never use formula past that date.3
  • Avoid tins that are dented, swollen, rusty or have a broken seal.
  • Store unopened tins in a cool, dry place indoors, not near the stove.3

Special formulas (lactose-free, partly broken-down protein, anti-reflux, preterm) are for specific medical reasons. Use them only when your doctor advises. Switching brands every week because of normal fussing usually makes things worse, not better.

Can I mix breast milk and formula?

Yes, many families combine breastfeeding with some formula, and any amount of breast milk is good for your baby.

The Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) advises exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months where possible.4 If you need formula too, protect your supply. Breast milk is made on demand: the more often milk is removed, the more you make.5 So offer the breast first at each feed, then top up with formula if needed.

It is better to give breast milk and formula separately rather than mixing them in one bottle. If your baby does not finish a mixed bottle, the precious breast milk is wasted too. Never use breast milk instead of water to make up formula powder.

How do I burp my baby, and is burping really needed?

Hold your baby upright against your shoulder, sitting on your lap, or face-down across your knees, and gently pat or rub her back.

Three common positions work well:

  • Over the shoulder: her chin rests on a cloth on your shoulder while you pat her back.
  • Sitting up: she sits on your lap, leaning forward, with your hand supporting her chest and chin.
  • Lying across your lap: tummy down, head slightly higher than her chest.

Burping is a family ritual, but the evidence is thin. A trial from Chandigarh found that burping did not reduce colic, and burped babies actually spat up more often.6 My advice: try a short burp if your baby seems uncomfortable or has gulped air. If nothing comes up after a minute or two, do not worry.

Can I give cow's milk or diluted milk instead of formula before one year?

No. Babies under 12 months should not drink plain cow's milk, buffalo milk or diluted milk as their main drink.7

Cow's milk has little iron. It can also cause tiny hidden blood losses from the gut in young babies, and its calcium and protein reduce iron absorption.8 It puts a heavy load of salts on immature kidneys, which is risky during diarrhoea.8 Diluting it with water does not fix these problems; it just makes the milk weaker.

If you cannot breastfeed, use a proper infant formula until the first birthday. If cost is a problem, tell your paediatrician honestly. We can plan together.

When should I see a doctor about formula feeding?

See a doctor the same day if your baby is feeding poorly, has fewer wet nappies, or seems unwell.

  • Fewer than 6 wet nappies a day after the first week, or dark yellow urine.
  • Refusing several feeds in a row, or very sleepy and hard to wake for feeds.
  • Forceful vomiting, green vomit, or blood in vomit or stool.
  • Fever of 38 degrees C or more in a baby under 3 months.
  • Rash, swelling of lips or face, or wheezing after a feed.
  • Not regaining birth weight by 2 weeks, or poor weight gain at check-ups.
  • Sunken soft spot on the head, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.

Go to hospital straight away if your baby is floppy, breathing fast or with effort, turning blue, or will not wake properly.
